# Design Evaluation Report – Evaluation 2

---

## My Objectives

- Verify whether the improvements added after Test 1 (interactive objects and optimized room layout) enhanced users’ communication fluency and sense of engagement.  
- Examine whether the newly added interactive tools, such as more manipulable furniture and decorative items, effectively addressed the previous lack of functionality.  
- Observe whether users’ sense of presence and immersion improved compared to Test 1.  

---

## Core Interview Questions

1. Compared to the previous version, do you feel communication in this space is more natural and fluent?  
2. Do you think the new interactive features (e.g., furniture placement, object rotation) are helpful?  
3. Do you feel that the new environment enhances your sense of immersion and presence?  
4. Are there any aspects that you still find confusing or unnecessary?  
5. Which areas would you most like to see improved in the next version?  

---

## Interview Summary (6 Participants Total)

### Communication Experience
- 5 participants felt interactions were more natural.  
- The added guiding lines made interactions more intuitive and reduced misunderstandings caused by overlapping objects.  

### Collaboration Features
- 4 participants found the new object interaction and remote grabbing features more practical.  
- 1 participant noted a minor bug where items shifted position during continuous grabbing, disrupting immersion.  

### Sense of Immersion and Presence
- 5 participants reported stronger immersion, especially when picking up small tabletop objects, which enhanced the overall atmosphere.  

### Remaining Issues
- Users suggested adding a **“backpack”** feature to manage furniture storage.  
- The **day–night switching function** still needs refinement.  

---

## Analysis

Compared with Test 1, users’ focus shifted from **“novelty”** to **“practicality.”**  
The primary theme centered on **functional personalization** — users began evaluating the experience from the perspectives of furniture interaction and tool support.  

The secondary theme was **visual consistency**, with repeated mentions of issues related to custom furniture and the number of available items.  

It is evident that the design focus has evolved from **basic furniture placement** toward **optimizing interactive experiences** and adding **new interaction features.**

---

## Concept Iteration Directions

### 1. Enhanced Interaction Features
Add an item backpack and provide users with multiple virtual furniture models to enable free combination and customization.

### 2. Optimized Visual Consistency
Unify lighting and material styles between XR and Unity environments to reduce visual fragmentation.

### 3. Tool Refinement
Improve interaction response speed and support the import of custom materials and models.

### 4. Increased Personalization Settings
Allow users to customize room layout and lighting to suit different scenarios.

---

## Reflection

The second round of testing shows that the core design concept has been validated.  
Users’ focus has shifted from **“interesting”** to **“practical.”**  
Communication and collaboration experiences have clearly improved.  

However, balancing the technical and perceptual differences between **MR** and **VR** remains a key challenge.  
Future development should focus on **enhancing visual realism** and **expressive facial interactions** to better support natural communication within mixed reality environments.
